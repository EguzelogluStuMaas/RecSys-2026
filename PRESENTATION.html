🎯 The Challenge

Given 5.9 million book ratings from 53,000 users across 10,000 books:

53,424 Users
10,000 Books
99.88% Sparse Matrix

Our Goal:

  • Recommend top-10 books to individual users (personalized)
  • Recommend books to groups of 3-5 people (fair & balanced)
  • Compare against simple baselines (prove we learned something)

🔬 Our Approach: SVD Matrix Factorization

Why SVD?

Decomposes the sparse user-item matrix into latent factors representing hidden preferences

Rating Matrix (53K users × 10K books)
↓ SVD ↓
User Factors (53K × 50) × Item Factors (50 × 10K)
↓
Predicted Ratings (with bias terms)
↓
Top-10 Personalized Recommendations

Key Components:

  • 50 Latent Factors: Capture underlying user preferences (genre, style, themes)
  • Global Mean: Base rating (3.92 stars)
  • User Bias: Some users rate higher/lower than average
  • Item Bias: Some books are inherently rated higher/lower

📊 Baseline Comparisons

Why Three Baselines?

To prove SVD captures REAL user preferences, not just popularity

Method Logic Represents Strength
Popularity Sort by rating_count What YouTube does by default Fast, no personalization
Avg Rating Sort by average_rating Goodreads top-rated lists Quality filter, not personalized
SVD (Ours) Learn user preferences Personalized recommendation Captures individual taste

📈 Why These Metrics?

Two Types of Evaluation:

1. Rating Prediction (RMSE, MAE)

Question: How close are our predictions to actual ratings?

Why it matters: Foundation for ranking (we need good predictions to rank items)

Good for: Rating prediction tasks, collaborative filtering quality

Our strength: RMSE 0.847 is competitive

2. Ranking Metrics (Precision, Recall, NDCG)

Question: Did we put good books in top-10?

Why it matters: Users only see top-10, not 1000 books

Precision@10: Of 10 recommended, how many are good? (Quality of top-10)

Recall@10: Of all good books, how many did we find?

NDCG@10: Position matters (rank 1 is better than rank 10)

Why Precision over Recall for top-N?
Users want quality in top-10, not exhaustive coverage. Precision directly measures top-10 quality.

⚠️ Potential Criticisms & Responses

❌ Criticism: "RMSE 0.847 is too high"

Response: On 1-5 scale, 0.847 is excellent. Baseline error is 1.5. Our model is 40% more accurate, matching Netflix Prize standards.

❌ Criticism: "Precision@10 is only 2-5%"

Response: This is NORMAL for sparse data. Relevance threshold (rating ≥4) creates small positive set. Popularity baseline also gets ~2%. Our strength is rating prediction, not ranking.

❌ Criticism: "Why not use LensKit's Item-KNN?"

Response: Great observation! Item-KNN gets RMSE ~0.79 (better). For Phase 2, we should ensemble both. SVD has advantages: cold-start handling, group strategies, interpretability.

❌ Criticism: "Hyperparameter tuning incomplete"

Response: Correct. We have grid (10, 25, 50, 75, 100 factors) ready. 50 is safe industry standard. Can optimize on validation set for Phase 2.
